# Japan vs US FDE: Funding, Mobility, and AI-Resilience Strategy

Answers from a planning conversation on the Japan-first vs US-first question, the obsolescence worry, and post-FDE exit roles.

---

## 1. Japan vs US — the Core Trade-off

| Dimension | Japan FDE | US FDE |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Compensation** | Lower base; top-tier global firms (OpenAI, Palantir) pay ¥15M–25M+ (see `japan-fde-career-takeaways.md`). | Significantly higher base + stock components. |
| **Savings velocity** | ~$30K/yr realistic to save → **5–8+ years** to reach the ~$240K–320K target. | **2–4 years** to reach the same target, even after higher COL. |
| **Job security** | Very high (Seishain protections, bilingual scarcity). | Marketable but more at-will. |

**Bottom line:** Japan = slower savings but secure, bilingual-moat path. US = fastest path to fund the adventure-retirement plan.

### The Bridge Strategy (best of both)
1. Work as an FDE in Japan for **6–7 years** (build track record + Japanese).
2. Then **move to the US** for **2–3 years** via internal transfer or new role.
3. This comfortably banks the $240K–320K travel nest egg by ~40.

## 2. How to Get to the US From Japan

- **Internal transfer (lateral)**: Highly feasible at multinationals with Japan offices — **Palantir** (pioneer of the role), **Google, Amazon, Microsoft** all support cross-region mobility after a solid track record.
- **New external application**: Possible after 6–7 years of FDE experience.
- **Master's degree fallback**: Doable but expensive (1–2 years of study time + tuition). Not the preferred route.

## 3. Will the FDE Role Become Obsolete With AI?

**No — not obsolete, expected to grow/evolve.** The role's core is exactly what AI can't replace:
- Human judgment in complex, ambiguous client environments.
- Problem-solving against messy, constrained, real-world enterprise systems.
- Integration expertise and high-touch client relationships.

> Don't stress about obsolescence — but do plan an **exit/adjacent role** that is even more communication-heavy.

## 4. Adjacent Roles That Emphasize Human Connection (More AI-Resistant)

If the priority shifts from pure code to **client handling + communication**, target these (listed roughly by fit):

1. **Solutions Architect** — designs large-scale cloud/data solutions; bridges tech and business.
2. **Sales Engineer** — technical validation during sales cycles; presentation-heavy.
3. **Technical Account Manager (TAM)** — long-term client success; relationship-driven.

These roles require understanding human context and organizational dynamics — highly resistant to automation — while keeping a technical foundation.

### Transferability note
- **FDE → SE/SA/TAM:** fluid — engineering credibility carries into commercial conversations.
- **SE → FDE:** a steep hill back (production-coding muscle atrophies). So the natural direction is FDE → communication-heavy roles.

## 5. Summary Decision Rules

- **Primary path:** Japan FDE → US bridge → save $240K–320K → retire ~40.
- **Do not** fear AI killing the FDE role; build communication as the durable moat.
- **Keep a hand** on Solutions Architect / Sales Engineer / TAM as the natural post-FDE or parallel track, especially for the Turing (brother's company) expansion plan.
