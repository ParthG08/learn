# Hobbies — Build vs. Avoid

**The golden rule:** *"Is this hobby making me a producer or a consumer?"* Producer hobbies compound. Consumer hobbies decay.

---

## Hobbies to Avoid (Consumer Traps)

| Hobby | Why It's a Waste |
|---|---|
| Watching live sports | Zero control, zero skill gain. You're a spectator. ~100 hours gone per tournament. |
| Binge-watching Netflix/Prime | You retain maybe 10% of it. 5 seasons = 50+ hours evaporated. |
| Scrolling Instagram / Reels / Shorts | Dopamine slots. Trains a 7-second attention span. Anti-productive. |
| Competitive video games | Grinding fake achievements in a fake world. No XP in real life. |
| Celebrity / political gossip | Emotional investment in people who don't know you exist. Zero ROI. |
| Social drinking | Costs money, damages health, gives fake confidence. |
| Mall / shopping as "leisure" | Paying to be sold things you don't need. You're a consumer, not a hobbyist. |
| Arguing in comment sections (Reddit, WhatsApp) | No one changes their mind. You just lose time. |

## Hobbies to Build (Producer Habits for an FDE)

### 1. Public Speaking & Storytelling
- **Why for FDE:** 50% of the job is explaining technical things to non-technical people. No story = no client.
- **How:** Join Toastmasters or start a YouTube channel explaining DevOps concepts in under 5 minutes. Record, watch back, cringe, improve.

### 2. Technical Writing / Blogging
- **Why for FDE:** FDEs write runbooks, deployment guides, architecture decision records. Clear writing = clear thinking.
- **How:** One deep-dive post every 2 weeks. "How I debugged a Kubernetes crash loop," etc.

### 3. Open-Source Contribution
- **Why for FDE:** FDEs land in unfamiliar client codebases daily. Contributing to OSS builds the muscle of diving into code you didn't write.
- **How:** Triage issues on projects like Apache Airflow, Kubernetes, or Terraform. Fix a small bug. Then handle a feature request. Then respond to a user's GitHub issue.

### 4. Infrastructure-as-Code Homelab
- **Why for FDE:** You deploy into client cloud environments. Must be fearless with AWS/GCP, Terraform, Ansible, Docker.
- **How:** Buy a cheap mini-PC or use a free tier account. Build a full CI/CD pipeline that deploys a dummy app. Break it. Fix it. Automate it. Do this for fun.

### 5. Teaching / Mentoring Juniors
- **Why for FDE:** FDEs train client teams post-deployment. Can you make a junior understand a complex topic in 20 minutes?
- **How:** Mentor a student on Discord/Reddit. The constraint forces you to simplify.

### 6. Reading Business & Finance Books
- **Why for FDE:** Clients operate on ROI, P&L, TCO. If you don't speak business, you're just a coder.
- **How:** "The Lean Startup," "Good to Great," "The Personal MBA." Read annual reports of public companies.

### 7. Heavy Resistance Training (Gym / Calisthenics)
- **Why for FDE:** Travel is brutal. Fitness = mental clarity = better on-site performance. You'll outlast your peers in client meetings.
- **How:** 3x/week compound lifts — squat, deadlift, bench, pull-ups. For stamina, not aesthetics.

### 8. Learning a Spoken Language
- **Why for FDE:** Big firms (Palantir, Datadog) have global clients. A second language makes you the go-to for EMEA or APAC accounts.
- **How:** 15 min/day Duolingo + shows in that language. 18 months to conversational. (Japanese is already in progress.)

### 9. Playing a Musical Instrument
- **Why:** Builds pattern recognition, discipline, and flow state — the same mental state needed for deep debugging.
- **How:** Used keyboard. Learn 3 chords. 20 min/day. Not about performing — about training focus.

### 10. Digital Fasts / Deliberate Boredom
- **Why for FDE:** FDEs sit in airports, hotel rooms, client lobbies. If you can't sit with your thoughts for 2 hours without a screen, you'll burn out.
- **How:** One weekend morning per month — no phone, no laptop, no music. Just sit, walk, or stare. Reclaim your attention span.

## Hobbies Under Debate — My Opinions

### Listening to Music (of all kinds)
**Verdict: Producer-leaning, with caution.**

Music is not passive consumption if you actively listen — analyzing structure, chord progressions, production techniques, or using it to enter flow state during deep work. It can fuel coding sessions and workouts. However, "having something on in the background 24/7" is a consumer pattern that ruins your ability to tolerate silence and think. Curate intentionally. Don't use it as a dopamine drip.

### Travelling
**Verdict: Producer, but only if done right.**

Travelling done well forces adaptability, problem-solving (missed flights, language barriers, unfamiliar systems), and perspective expansion — all FDE-relevant skills. The caveat: if it's purely "checklist tourism" (photo at landmark, move on), it's just another form of consumption. Travel with intent — talk to locals, solve real problems on the road, document what you learn. The goal is exposure, not escapism.

### Dancing
**Verdict: Strong producer activity.**

Dancing is high-agency physical expression. It builds body awareness, confidence, rhythm (pattern recognition), social skills, and presence — you cannot dance well while scrolling your phone. It's a forced digital detox and a workout simultaneously. Salsa, bachata, hip-hop — whatever form — it trains you to be in your body, not just your head. FDEs need that balance.

### Playing Sports (Investing Time to Be Decent)
**Verdict: Strong producer activity — with a volume cap.**

Playing sports is one of the highest-ROI physical hobbies you can have. Gym builds raw strength and stamina. Sport adds layers gym doesn't touch: **strategic thinking under pressure** (reading plays, anticipating opponents, split-second decisions — the same mental circuit you fire during a production incident), **teamwork and communication** (coordinating under fatigue), **resilience through failure** (you lose matches, make errors, get back up), and **deliberate practice toward a measurable skill** (getting better at a serve, a shot, a move). That deliberate-practice mindset is the exact same muscle you use to improve at coding, debugging, or system design.

The fact that you're investing real effort to be *decent* — not just showing up casually — puts this firmly in producer territory. You're building a skill, not consuming entertainment.

**The "multiple sports" concern:** This is a time-management problem, not a hobby-quality problem. Playing 2-3 sports at a serious level can easily eat 12-18 hours/week with practice, travel, and recovery. That competes directly with career-building time. The fix is not to quit sports — it's to cap volume. One primary sport + one secondary for cross-training is sustainable. Three at high intensity is not (unless you're a professional athlete, which you're not).

**The injury risk is real** — higher than gym training — and an injury that sidelines you for weeks impacts everything else on this roadmap. Train smart. Warm up. Don't play through pain.

**Bottom line:** Sports = producer. Multiple sports = fine with a time cap. Playing sports well enough to be "the decent player" = high-agency, compound-growth activity. Just don't let it become the main thing unless it *is* the main thing.
