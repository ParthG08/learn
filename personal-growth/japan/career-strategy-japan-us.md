# Japan Career Strategy — US Detour & Japan's Economy (short notes)

Key takeaways from the career-strategy conversation.

## 1. Long-Term Career Arbitrage (Japan → US → Japan)

- **Path:** Japan (learn Japanese + technical + customer-facing depth) → US for 2–4 years at a top AI/tech company → return to Japan at a much higher level.
- **Comp gap (2026):**
  - Senior US AI-engineer TC ≈ **$335k**; staff ≈ **$445k**.
  - Japan AI-engineer senior median ≈ **¥27M**, upper quartile ≈ **¥33M**.
  - Tokyo general backend ≈ **¥10.5M** median, **¥15M** at 75th pct.
- **Why the US detour:** higher savings + world's strongest AI ecosystem + stronger resume when returning to Japan (the resume part may matter more than money).
- **Timeline:** 2026–29/30 Japan → 2029/30–2032/33 US → 2032/33+ return to Japan.
- **Target profile (not "7 yrs Japanese SWE"):** US-trained AI engineer + excellent software + Japanese/English bilingual + enterprise problem-solver + customer-facing technical leader → premium in Japan (¥30M+ potential in 2030s).
- **Big caveat:** don't go to US just because it's the US.
  - Japan ¥12M + excellent AI company + customer exposure > US $160k + boring CRUD work.
  - US $250–350k+ + serious AI company > Japan ¥12–18M generic role (if visa/immigration workable).
  - US has more employment/visa risk for foreigners (H-1B layoffs are painful).
- **Priority order to optimize:** 1) technical excellence 2) AI/ML + distributed systems 3) customer-facing 4) Japanese/English 5) globally recognized companies 6) compensation. Compensation follows the combination.
- Consider **3–5 years in US**, not just 2, if learning/network/equity are exceptional.

## 2. Bilingual Advantage for FDE in Japan

- **Yes, significantly easier** if Japanese is genuinely business-level.
- FDE needs: direct client/executive communication, translating business problems → technical solutions, bridging Japanese + global engineering teams.
- Exceptional engineer + J/E bilingual in Japan → **strong positioning** (few competitors).
- Exceptional engineer without the language edge in India → more competition for fewer true FDE roles.

## 3. "Japan Is Dying" Narrative — Reality Check

- **Real problems:** working-age population shrinking ~0.6%/yr, aging, very high public debt, IMF baseline GDP growth ~0.6%/yr to 2031. Not a high-growth economy.
- **Logical jump to avoid:** population/economy shrinking ≠ no good jobs/high salaries.
- **The counter-argument:** Japan's core problem is a **labor shortage** (highest in decades, per IMF), pushing automation/AI adoption. Scarce, globally valuable talent becomes **more** valuable, not less.
- A bilingual AI/FDE is not competing for average Japanese jobs — it's a scarce role in a labor-constrained economy.

### Probability assessment
- No good tech jobs in Japan by 2035 → ❌ very unlikely.
- ¥30M becomes impossible → ❌ very unlikely.
- ¥30M stays an elite salary → ✅ very likely.
- Strong bilingual AI/FDE earning ¥30M+ in their 40s → ✅ plausible.
- Leave Japan only because population declines → ❌ wouldn't.
- Keep the option to move to US/global → ✅ absolutely.

### 2035 base case
Slow-growth, older, increasingly automated, labor-constrained, but still wealthy. Ordinary jobs stay low-paying; globally valuable tech jobs get more differentiated/expensive — good for this strategy.

- **Bigger risk than Japan's economy:** skill commoditization. "Go backend engineer" = vulnerable. "AI/FDE who deploys AI into real organizations across Japan/US" = hard to commoditize.
- **Final stance:** keep Japan as home + build skills valuable internationally; maintain flexibility to work globally. Don't conflate macro weakness with an individual's prospects.
